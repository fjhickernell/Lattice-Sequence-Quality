# Reading copies for the revision notes

Inventory of the 12 references cited in `author-notes/revision-proposal/main.tex`.
As checked on November 4, 2026, all twelve reading copies are present.
Saved PDF titles, authors and page counts were checked; availability
does not imply that each paper's methods have received a complete review.

## Public repository and local reading copies

For the nine additional references in this update, KKS19 carries an explicit
[Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 license](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/)
and its unchanged PDF is included with attribution and source below.
The other eight new PDFs are retained locally and excluded from Git because
public redistribution permission was not established. Their local links
work when the reading copies are present; the source links provide legitimate
access routes on another machine, sometimes requiring library authentication.
The three previously tracked reading copies retain their existing status.

In particular, free-to-read publisher or author hosting is not itself a
redistribution license. Madsen83 uses the
[Elsevier User License](https://www.elsevier.com/about/policies-and-standards/open-access-licenses/elsevier-user),
and TAK07 was retrieved through a library subscription. JH19 and KKSS26 use
arXiv's nonexclusive distribution license, which
[does not grant further reuse permission](https://info.arxiv.org/help/license/reuse.html).
Any separately retained author-sharing rights would need to be verified before
adding those local-only copies to this public repository.

**TAK07 — N. L. Tsitsas, E. G. Alivizatos and G. H. Kalogeropoulos (2007).**
*A recursive algorithm for the inversion of matrices with circulant blocks.*
Applied Mathematics and Computation 188, 877–894.
[DOI: 10.1016/j.amc.2006.10.044](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.amc.2006.10.044).
Local-only file: [Tsitsas_Alivizatos_Kalogeropoulos_2007_Recursive_Circulant_Blocks.pdf](Tsitsas_Alivizatos_Kalogeropoulos_2007_Recursive_Circulant_Blocks.pdf),
18 pages, published version. Source: the publisher's
[article page](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0096300306014275),
downloaded through Illinois Tech's subscribed ScienceDirect access in Safari.
The PDF header, title, authors, DOI and page count were verified locally.
Preliminary comparison: printed pp. 879–882 Fourier-diagonalize complete,
equal-size circulant blocks and recursively apply Schur-complement inverse
formulas. For an m-by-m block array with dyadic block size N, Theorem 8
(p. 888) gives O(m³N + m²N log N) arithmetic. This is direct antecedent for
FFT-plus-Schur computation; the notes' selector-coupled arbitrary-prefix
recursion needs additional arguments and a detailed prior-art comparison.
