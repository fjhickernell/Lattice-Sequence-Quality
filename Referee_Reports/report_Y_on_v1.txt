The paper investigates the convergence of the classical figure of merit P_\alpha for extensible lattice rules. Extensible lattice rules have previously been introduced by Hickernell and Niederreiter. In that paper the authors studied the convergence rates when the number of points is of the form N = b^m. The present paper asks what happens to the worst-case error (i.e. P_\alpha), when N is of a more general form. It is known that in general this limits the convergence rate (for instance consider f(x) = \exp(2\pi i x_1) for x = (x_1, x_2, ..., x_s). Then the first b^m points yield integration error 0, whereas N = b^m+1 points yield error \exp(2\pi i x_{b^m,1}) / (b^m+1). Hence in general we cannot expect a better rate than O(1/N) for all N \in \mathbb{N}.

The proofs in this paper are therefore not surprising and are relatively straight forward to derive from the known literature. In my opinion this does not reach the level sufficient for publication in J. Complexity.
